1 The Game Rules

Five vocabulary words and you're 80% there.

Think of Cru.org as a giant board game. Every day, thousands of people show up and start playing. Analytics is just the scoreboard — it tells you who came, what they tried, and which paths actually led somewhere meaningful.

Players

Users

The unique people who show up. One person on a laptop and the same person on their phone usually count as two users. Useful for understanding reach.

Visits

Sessions

One sitting on the site. A session ends after 30 minutes of inactivity or when the user leaves. One user can have many sessions over time.

Moves

Events

Anything a user does — viewing a page, clicking a button, scrolling, watching a video, submitting a form. GA4 logs everything as an event.

Winning Moves

Conversions

The events you've decided actually matter — email sign-ups, donation clicks, "Find a Cru near me." You choose which moves count.

Entry Paths

Traffic Sources

How people got here: Google search, social media, a typed URL, a newsletter link, a paid ad. Different doors mean different player intent.

2 The Strategy Loop

Four questions you'll come back to every time.

Beginners get lost in GA4 because there are so many menus. Avoid that by always running the same loop. This is the "how to think" piece — it sits above any specific report.

1

What is happening?

Start at the metric. Up, down, flat compared to last month / last year? Don't try to explain anything yet — just describe it.

Example"Email sign-ups jumped 29% after the landing page redesign."
2

Where is it happening?

Narrow the camera. Which page? Which device? Which traffic source? Which audience segment? "Where" usually flips a vague stat into a useful one.

Example"Most of the lift is coming from mobile users arriving via organic search."
3

Why might it be happening?

Now you can guess. GA4 will rarely tell you "why" outright — your job is to combine data with what you know about the redesign, the campaign, the season.

Example"The new page added context above the form. Mobile users were the ones bouncing before. Makes sense the lift shows up there."
4

What move should we make next?

This is where analytics becomes useful. Translate the insight into one decision — a test, a content tweak, a stakeholder conversation.

Example"Apply the same context-rich pattern to the volunteer signup form, where mobile bounce is also high."

A page with high views but low engagement time is a "headline that overpromised." A page with fewer views but high engagement is a hidden gem worth promoting.

  2. Default grouping is "Session default channel group"

  4. Sort by Engaged sessions to find the most-effective doors

Email and direct traffic almost always engage more than social. If a redesigned page is getting most of its traffic from social, expect surface-level skimming — design accordingly.

  2. Pick the conversion: email_signup, give_click, connect_lead
  3. Use the secondary dimension to break it down by Page path or Source / medium

A 5% lift in traffic that produces a 30% lift in conversions means the new traffic is higher-intent — that's a stronger result than the headline number suggests.

  1. Open any report (Pages, Traffic, etc.)
  2. Click the date range in the top-right corner
  3. Toggle Compare on
  4. Pick "Same period last year" — it accounts for seasonality

The single most important habit on this list. Numbers in isolation are nearly meaningless. Always answer "compared to what?"

4 Story Mode

Each redesign initiative, translated into a story about real people.

Nine areas of the Cru.org redesign — and what their numbers are actually telling you about visitor behavior.

Clear win

Homepage

Engagement edged up. Pathway traffic +20%.

Visitors aren't loitering on the homepage — they're using it as a doorway and walking through. That's exactly what a homepage is supposed to do.

Move: Stop measuring homepage success by time-on-page. Measure it by downstream click-through into Learn / Connect / Go.

Clear win

Navigation & Give

Give CTA engagement doubled.

When you make the door visible and put a few hand-picked invitations next to it, more people walk through. The path got clearer, not the urge stronger.

Move: Treat the featured promo slots as scarce, valuable real estate. Rotate deliberately and measure each one.

Clear win

Email Subscriptions

+29% conversion · −57% bounce.

The old page was asking for an email before earning trust. The new page explains the offer, then asks. That sequence change is doing all the work.

Move: Audit every "ask" page on the site for the same pattern: context first, request second.

Clear win

Connect Tool

2,200+ users. Real leads flowing.

A specific, location-aware tool beats a generic "Contact Us." When the site can answer "is there something for me, near me?" — people use it.

Move: Look for other places where a generic page could become a "for you, near you" tool.

Clear win

Audience Pages

34K+ visits across 6 audiences.

People self-select into identities (athlete, parent, professional). When the site offers a page that reflects them, they engage. Search alone can't do this.

Move: Watch which audience pages overperform and build deeper journeys for those audiences first.

Mixed signal

Learn Section

Active users +12.8% · Pageviews −8.2% YoY.

More humans, fewer pages each. Two reads: the new search/filter is helping people find what they want faster (good), or related content is harder to discover (worth investigating).

Move: Pull engagement time per session. If session length is steady, the redesign is winning. If down, surface related content more.

Mixed signal

Go Section

Mission −10–15% · Internships +19.5%.

A reshuffle, not a loss. Visitors are sorting themselves into more accurate buckets — short-term commitment vs. career path — instead of all funneling through "mission trips" by default.

Move: Track downstream conversions (applications, info-session signups) before drawing conclusions.

Watch closely

About Section

Trust → action funnel still being captured.

An About section is rarely a destination. It's a "I'm checking if this is a real, trustworthy organization" stop. Engagement here predicts trust further down the funnel.

Move: Track whether visitors who land on About are more likely to give, subscribe, or connect afterward.

Quiet win

Video Pages

22 videos · ~300 monthly visits avg.

Steady, not spectacular — but compounding. Video adds dwell time and gives Google more reason to surface the page. The first and most-recent videos behave very differently in GA4.

Move: Compare top-vs-bottom video performance. If the gap is huge, the difference is probably topic or thumbnail, not template.

5 Practice — Unlock the Insight

Seven small puzzles to train the muscle.

Try answering each before revealing. The reasoning matters more than the exact answer — these scenarios show up in real Cru.org review meetings.

1

The most popular page

Pages and screens shows that /us/en/how-to-know-god has by far the highest views on Cru.org. What does that tell you about visitor intent — and why should the redesign team care?

Insight unlocked

A huge chunk of Cru.org visitors are showing up with a single, urgent question — not browsing the brand. They're looking for an answer to a deeply personal question.

Why it matters: The page's job isn't to "convert" in a marketing sense. Its job is to be clear, calming, and trustworthy in the first 5 seconds. Engagement time, scroll depth, and onward navigation are the metrics that actually count.

2

The drop-off mystery

A landing page shows 10,000 sessions, 8% engagement rate, and 3 conversions. What's the most likely problem, and what would you check next?

Insight unlocked

The page is getting plenty of traffic but visitors are leaving almost immediately. That's a mismatch problem, not a content problem.

Check next: (1) Traffic source — paid/social often deliver mismatched intent. (2) Headline above the fold — does it match what was promised? (3) Mobile vs. desktop split. Fix the mismatch, not the page.

3

Two doors, two players

Organic search brings 60% of homepage traffic; email brings 8%. But email visitors convert at 4× the rate. Which channel matters more, and what should you do about it?

Insight unlocked

Both matter, for different reasons. Organic = reach. Email = depth. They aren't competing — they're a relay race.

The move: Use organic-search content to capture emails. Then let email do the converting work. The story isn't "email beats search" — it's "search fills the funnel that email later closes."

4

The misleading dip

Mission trip page views dropped 10–15% after the Go section relaunch. A stakeholder calls this a failure. Is it? What additional data would you bring to the meeting?

Insight unlocked

Not necessarily a failure. Internships and jobs both rose. Visitors who were on the wrong page before are now on the right page.

Bring to the meeting: (1) Total Go-section traffic. (2) Conversion rate per page — applications, info-session signups. (3) Time-to-conversion. If overall outcomes are up, the dip is a sorting effect, not a loss.

5

Engagement vs. pageviews

The Learn section saw +12.8% active users but −8.2% pageviews YoY. Same site. What story explains both?

Insight unlocked

More people are showing up, but each person is viewing fewer pages. Most likely: the new search and filter UI is helping people find what they want faster.

How to confirm: Check average engagement time per session. If it's holding steady or rising, the redesign is genuinely more efficient. If session time is also down, content discoverability slipped — surface "related articles" more aggressively.

6

The "doubled CTA"

Give CTA clicks doubled after the navigation redesign. A teammate says "donations should follow." What would you watch for, and what could go wrong with that prediction?

Insight unlocked

A click on the CTA isn't a donation. It's intent. The two metrics can move in opposite directions if the giving page itself has friction.

Watch: Click-to-donation conversion rate. If clicks doubled but donations rose only 30%, the giving page is now the bottleneck. The CTA worked; the next page didn't keep up.

7

Audience pages, asymmetric

Across the six audience pages, the Athletes page gets 5× the visits of the Pastors page. Does that mean the Pastors page is failing?

Insight unlocked

No — and judging audience pages on raw traffic is the wrong scoreboard. A page reaching 200 highly relevant pastors a month is more valuable than one reaching 1,000 mildly curious athletes.

Better metric: Engagement rate, conversion rate, and downstream actions per audience. Each audience page has its own ceiling — measure it against itself over time, not against the others.

Pocket Glossary

The fewest GA4 terms you can get away with. Skim once, refer back when reports look intimidating.

Active Users
People who actually engaged in the time period (not just loaded a page and left).
Engagement Rate
% of sessions that lasted 10+ seconds, had a conversion, or 2+ pageviews.
Engaged Sessions
The actual count behind engagement rate. Use when comparing channels.
Avg. Engagement Time
How long a typical visitor was actively focused on the site.
Key Event / Conversion
An event you've flagged as important. You decide which events count.
Source / Medium
Where + how. e.g. google / organic, newsletter / email.
Channel Group
Bucket of similar source/mediums: Organic, Direct, Email, Social, Paid.
Page Path
The URL after the domain — /us/en/learn. Filter reports to a section.
Landing Page
The first page someone saw in their session. The "front door" for that visit.
Exploration
GA4's advanced playground. Funnels, paths, free-form analyses. Graduate to it.
